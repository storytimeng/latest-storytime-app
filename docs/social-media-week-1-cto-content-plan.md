## Day 4 — Thursday: “Under the hood” (CTO build-in-public)

**Theme:** Technical credibility + curiosity — architecture, desktop dashboard, scale  
**Best platforms:** LinkedIn, X, dev-community cross-post (optional)

### LinkedIn post

**Hook:** We shipped two products in one codebase.

**Body:**

> A quick CTO transparency post on how Storytime is built:
>
> **1. Mobile shell (PWA)** — `/home`, `/library`, `/write` … optimised for phone-width, installable, fast iteration.
>
> **2. Desktop dashboard** — `/app/*` — sidebar, library grids, writer tools for tablet & laptop users without breaking the mobile experience.
>
> Same auth. Same stories. Same API. Two intentional experiences.
>
> Why? Because **mobile-first doesn’t mean mobile-only.** Readers on iPad and writers on laptop deserve a native-feeling layout too.
>
> Stack: Next.js, TypeScript, PWA, API-backed content, subscription & ambassador systems live.
>
> **App stores:** packaging the mobile PWA into native wrappers — deployment in progress.
>
> If you’re a dev or product person, I’m happy to share what we learned building dual-shell architecture in public.

**CTA:** Engineers & PMs — what would you want to see in a storytelling app? Comment below.

### X thread (technical but accessible)

1. How do you ship a storytelling app mobile-first _and_ desktop-ready without two codebases?
2. Answer: one API, two shells. Mobile PWA + `/app` desktop dashboard.
3. Middleware routes you to the right experience. Your preference sticks.
4. Mobile ships first. Always. Desktop catches power users.
5. storytime.ng — try both on your devices. Store apps soon.

Simple architecture diagram (can be hand-drawn or Excalidraw):
`Reader/Writer` → `Mobile PWA` | `Desktop /app` → `Storytime API` → `Stories, Genres, Premium, Ambassadors`
